# ADR 0009: Model BARCAP as spatial defensive barriers

## Status

Accepted

Station orientation, defended-side candidate selection, and defensive-capacity reinforcement were amended by ADR 0013.

## Context

The original BARCAP request treated hostile air-interference areas and friendly airfields as circular patrol objectives. It could concentrate a full requested formation over one location while leaving another approach uncovered, duplicate an airport patrol even when a forward patrol already screened that airport, and authorize nearby opposing patrols to engage without either crossing the other's defensive line. A fixed mission radius also made aircraft with very different interception performance provide equivalent coverage.

## Decision

BARCAP planning materializes an ordered friendly-side barrier with a representative hostile source and protected-asset counts. Primary threat sources are remembered hostile airborne combat presence and functional hostile airports with apparently available combat-capable aircraft. Front-line divisions are protected first, with separate barriers for materially different hostile approach sectors. Every friendly-controlled airport is then checked by tracing hostile-to-airport lanes; an existing barrier that blocks a lane receives protection credit, and a supplemental barrier is created only for an uncovered lane. Active military airports have greater demand value than empty, damaged, or otherwise reserve airports. If primary threat information is empty, all known hostile airports orient front-line barriers but do not bootstrap separate airport patrols; hostile-controlled ground supplies a final direction fallback when no hostile airport exists. A hostile reference that falls on the protected line is replaced by an external hostile-controlled reference; a barrier without a valid hostile-to-friendly direction is not generated. Disconnected portions of an ordered front become separate barrier runs rather than being joined across hostile or neutral territory. Isolated protected assets may produce a one-tile local barrier.

BARCAP fulfillment is spatial rather than formation-strength based. Each package assigns one combat-capable aircraft to the center of the largest uncovered barrier run. The protected tile line creates a threat-facing weapon-release deadline ten nautical miles away, clamped before the hostile reference when the campaign tile scale is smaller than that standoff. For each aircraft and planned loadout, the package builder searches continuous world-space positions in a defended-side wedge and chooses the deepest route- and fuel-feasible station that preserves the same uncovered coverage; if the preferred station cannot support one circuit, the builder retries shallower stations and other squadrons. Each tile's credited coverage conservatively includes maximum racetrack displacement and is limited by aircraft combat speed, planned air-to-air launch range and preparation time, representative hostile speed, time from the hostile source to the release line, and warning actually available from functioning friendly IADS radar coverage along that approach. Projected committed coverage is unioned tile by tile across requests and planning cycles only when the old and new threat-facing directions are compatible. An active patrol stops receiving its originally planned coverage credit if expenditure or aircraft loss removes the weapon range used to plan that coverage. Request ordering favors the least-covered barriers, spreading scarce aircraft before reinforcing covered positions. A fulfilled spatial request is reopened if cancellation, abort, capability loss, or early recovery creates a later gap; completing one patrol rotation does not complete the sustained barrier request.

During execution, a materialized BARCAP monitors hostile tracks whose projected courses cross its assigned weapon-release segment. It commits only when hostile time to the crossing is no greater than the patrol's current time to effective launch geometry plus a ninety-second margin. Live interception time accounts for current position, combat speed, remaining air-to-air weapon range, and preparation time. A hostile that has already penetrated the segment remains an immediate target within the aircraft-specific response depth. Nose-on geometry alone does not make an aircraft remaining on the hostile side a BARCAP self-defense target. The defended normal is derived from the unshifted protected line rather than the release-line offset, preventing a short threat distance from reversing sides. Invalid or degenerate direction data fails closed and cannot authorize an engagement. Every BARCAP tactical aim point is clamped to the defended side of the release line, including intercept, launch-support, and defensive maneuvers, so an engagement cannot drag the patrol through its screen. A single covered tile is treated as a short local barrier segment. Legacy or authored BARCAP routes without barrier metadata retain bounded circular authorization.

The campaign map renders each alliance's threat-facing weapon-release lines and brighter portions covered by committed or active flights. The display can be toggled independently from flight routes.

## Consequences

BARCAP is defensive by geometry and timing: opposing patrols station as far rearward as their demonstrated sensor-to-shooter response permits and do not automatically hunt one another, while an aircraft approaching a protected asset's release line is committed against only at the latest safe time. Forward barriers can satisfy downstream airport protection, preventing duplicate coverage. Faster and longer-range aircraft can station farther back when the friendly IADS can warn them in time, and scarce aircraft are distributed across open approaches.

CAS and strike packages are intended future protected assets. They are not represented by placeholder data or speculative request logic until those missions exist.
